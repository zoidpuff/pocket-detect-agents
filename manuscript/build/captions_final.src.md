@@@CAPTION fig1_pipeline
title: The BURROW pipeline.
text: Top row: analytical geometry. Only protein atoms are kept (ligands, waters and ions are removed). In `--af-mode`, low-confidence segments (pLDDT < 50) are removed and atoms get soft occlusion weights, w = clip((pLDDT − 40)/40, 0, 1). Candidate volume elements come from a 1 Å solvent-shell grid that is filtered by a LIGSITE protein–solvent–protein (PSP) test, plus metal-coordination candidates. Each point is described by ray-cast buriedness at several ray lengths (through a full-atom grid and a backbone-only grid), first-hit chemistry, densities of atom and residue classes, weighted contact number (WCN) and protrusion. A LightGBM classifier scores the points. Bottom row: candidate pockets are formed by single linkage at several thresholds of buriedness and point score, with control of chaining. A LightGBM ranker that was trained on cross-fitted point scores ranks them, so that scores are comparable across proteins. A pocket is suppressed if its centre lies within 10 Å of a better-ranked pocket or if it overlaps one. The v2 output floor then drops pockets that score below 0.03 once three have been kept. Blue boxes are learned steps. Parameters are those of the final version (v2, default preset).
@@@END

@@@CAPTION fig2_ligysis
title: Pocket prediction on the LIGYSIS test set (2,775 chains, 6,721 binding sites).
text: (A) Top-N+2 success: a site counts as found when the pocket closest to its centre is within 12 Å (DCC) and has a rank of N+2 or better, where N is the number of sites on the chain (pre-registered endpoint C1). (B) The same with a stricter criterion for a hit: distance to any ligand atom (DCA) ≤ 4 Å (6,387 sites with ligand atoms; C1-strict). (C) Pooled precision P1K (endpoint C2): the share of true positives among the 1,000 highest-scoring pockets pooled over all chains. Right column: mean number of pockets that each method outputs per chain. Because the closest-pocket rule gives less credit to long output lists, top-N+2 must be read together with list length (see Fig 4). Points: seed 0 for BURROW. Bars: 95% paired cluster-bootstrap CIs (30% sequence clusters, 1,687 units, 1,000 resamples), shown where a CI was computed. "Re-run here": P2Rank 2.5.1 and fpocket 4.2.3 + PRANK 2.4.2, run on identical inputs. "Published predictions": the LIGYSIS authors' per-chain predictions, scored with our evaluation code, which reproduces their published numbers to within 0.4 points for all 33 method variants. For these, no CIs or pocket counts were computed, and chains without predictions count as misses. The fast preset was not pre-registered (descriptive).
@@@END

@@@CAPTION fig4_composition_tradeoff
title: What drives the LIGYSIS margin: output length and ligand class.
text: (A) LIGYSIS recall under four matching rules (seed 0). Closest-pocket top-N+2 is the LIGYSIS protocol (endpoint C1): a site is credited only to the pocket nearest to its centre. Any-pocket: any of the top N+2 pockets within 12 Å. One-to-one: Hungarian matching of pockets to sites. Maximum recall: any pocket in the output. Bars: 95% cluster-bootstrap CIs (not computed for the published P2Rank predictions). (B) Mean number of pockets output per chain (not available for the published P2Rank predictions). The v2 output floor shortens the list. This raises closest-pocket top-N+2 and lowers coverage (any-pocket, one-to-one and maximum recall), so the C1 gain from v1 to v2 is an effect of output length rather than of better ranking. (C, D) Differences in top-N+2 by ligand class. (C) LIGYSIS ion, nucleotide-cofactor and other sites: pre-registered descriptive strata of C1 (`all` ranker) against our re-run of fpocket+PRANK. LIGYSIS non-ion (†, post hoc): the LIGYSIS site set without ions, with the `small` ranker. (D) AlphaFold set (†, post hoc, v2 only) against P2Rank's AlphaFold configuration; non-ion means nucleotide-cofactor plus other. Open markers: post-hoc analyses (not pre-registered). n, scored sites. Bars: 95% paired cluster-bootstrap CIs.
@@@END
