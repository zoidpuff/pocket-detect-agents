## Fig1 — `fig1_pipeline.pdf` / `.png`

**Fig 1. BURROW pipeline.** Top row: analytical geometry. Protein atoms only (ligands, waters and ions removed); in `--af-mode`, low-confidence segments (pLDDT < 50) are pruned and atoms get soft occlusion weights, w = clip((pLDDT − 40)/40, 0, 1). Candidate volume elements come from a 1 Å solvent-shell grid filtered by a LIGSITE protein–solvent–protein (PSP) cascade, plus metal-coordination candidates. Each point is described by ray-cast buriedness at several ray lengths (through a full-atom grid and a backbone-only grid), first-hit chemistry, atom- and residue-class densities, weighted contact number (WCN) and protrusion. A LightGBM classifier scores the points. Bottom row: candidate pockets are formed by single linkage at several buriedness and point-score thresholds, with chaining control. They are ranked by a LightGBM ranker trained on cross-fitted point scores, which gives scores that are comparable across proteins. Pockets whose centres lie within 10 Å of a better-ranked pocket, or that overlap it, are suppressed. The v2 output floor then drops pockets scoring below 0.03 once three are kept. Blue boxes are learned steps. Parameters as in docs/METHOD.md (FREEZE v2, default preset).

## Fig2 — `fig2_ligysis.pdf` / `.png`

**Fig 2. Pocket prediction on the LIGYSIS test set (2,775 chains, 6,721 binding sites).** (A) Top-N+2 success: a site counts as found when the pocket closest to its centre is within 12 Å (DCC) and ranks within N+2, where N is the number of sites on the chain (pre-registered endpoint C1). (B) The same with a stricter hit, distance to any ligand atom (DCA) ≤ 4 Å (6,387 sites with ligand atoms; C1-strict). (C) Pooled precision P1K (endpoint C2): the share of true positives among the 1,000 highest-scoring pockets pooled over all chains. Right column: mean pockets output per chain. The closest-pocket rule penalises long output lists, so top-N+2 must be read together with list length (see Fig 4). Points: seed 0 (BURROW). Bars: 95 % paired cluster-bootstrap CIs (30 % sequence clusters, 1,687 units, 1,000 resamples), shown where a CI was computed. 'Re-run here': P2Rank 2.5.1 and fpocket 4.2.3 + PRANK 2.4.2 run by us on identical inputs. 'Published predictions': the LIGYSIS authors' per-chain predictions, rescored by our harness, which reproduces their published numbers within 0.38 points (gate ±0.5) for all 33 method variants (results/validation/ligysis_reproduction.md); for these no CIs or pockets per chain were computed, and chains without predictions count as misses. The fast preset is not pre-registered (descriptive). Sources: results/test/v{1,2}/summary.json, results/test/v2/DESCRIPTIVE.md, results/baselines/HEADLINES.md, results/validation/ligysis_reproduction/reproduction.csv.

## Fig4 — `fig4_composition_tradeoff.pdf` / `.png`

**Fig 4. What drives the LIGYSIS margin: output length and ligand class.** (A) LIGYSIS recall under four matching rules (seed 0). Closest-pocket top-N+2 is the LIGYSIS protocol (endpoint C1): a site is credited only to the pocket nearest its centre. Any-pocket: any of the top N+2 pockets within 12 Å. One-to-one: Hungarian matching of pockets to sites. Max recall: any output pocket. Bars: 95 % cluster-bootstrap CIs (not computed for the published P2Rank predictions, which come from results/validation/ligysis_reproduction/reproduction.csv). (B) Mean pockets output per chain (not available for the published P2Rank predictions). The v2 output floor shortens the list, which raises closest-pocket top-N+2 and lowers coverage (any-pocket, one-to-one, max recall), so the C1 gain from v1 to v2 is an output-length effect rather than better ranking (REPORT.md §4.2). (C, D) Δ top-N+2 by ligand class. (C) LIGYSIS ion, nucleotide-cofactor and other: pre-registered descriptive strata of C1 (`all` ranker) vs our fpocket+PRANK re-run. LIGYSIS non-ion (†, post-hoc): the LIGYSIS_NI site set with the `small` ranker. (D) AF set (†, post-hoc, v2 only) vs P2Rank -c alphafold; non-ion = nucleotide-cofactor + other. Open markers: post-hoc analyses (not pre-registered). n = scored sites. Bars: 95 % paired cluster-bootstrap CIs. Sources: results/test/v{1,2}/summary.json (secondary, strata), results/test/v2/posthoc.json.

(C) Throughput of the BURROW CLI over 10,000 structures with 24 CPU workers or 3 GPUs with 8 CPU workers each, including start-up, parsing and writing (docs/PERF.md, Track T1, 'after' column). Shared node: other users' jobs ran concurrently.

## Fig6 — `fig6_af_dimers.pdf` / `.png`

**Fig 6. AlphaFold models and protein dimers.** (A) AF set (2,526 AlphaFold models, 6,502 sites, 1,691 cluster units). Left: top-N+2 (DCC ≤ 12 Å). Right: share of top-N+2 pockets that are hallucinated, i.e. whose mean pLDDT is < 50 and that match no site within 12 Å (pooled; no CI). BURROW rows: v2 `all` default model, seed 0, with naive input (no AF handling), with naive input plus removal of pockets with mean pLDDT < 70, and in `--af-mode` (the pre-registered O5 variant). The naive and filter rows are descriptive and have CIs only for their paired differences vs AF mode (results/test/v2/DESCRIPTIVE.md). Bars on points: 95 % cluster-bootstrap CIs. (B) Dimer benchmark (400 assemblies, 807 unique sites, of which 243 lie at an interface). Filled: full-assembly input (site level, with 95 % CI). Open: every chain predicted alone. In chain mode the unit is a chain copy and a site is scored on each chain unit that carries it (634 interface and 1,678 total rows), so the two input modes have different denominators. Counting a site as found when any isolated chain finds it, BURROW's interface DCC12 top-N+2 changes by −1.2 [−7.2, +4.4] pp vs assembly input (DCA4: −11.1 [−16.2, −5.6] pp). Isolated-chain rows are descriptive. Sources: results/test/v2/summary.json (secondary: af_set, dimer), results/test/v2/DESCRIPTIVE.md.
